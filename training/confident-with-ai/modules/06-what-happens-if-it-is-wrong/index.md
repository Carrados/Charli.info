Part Two: Judge What AI Gives You

Use green, amber and red risk bands to decide how much checking is proportionate.

**Estimated time:** 10 minutes. **Last reviewed:** 11 August 2026.

## What you need to know

This page stands alone. No previous AI knowledge is required. You need access to an AI assistant only for the activity.

**Learning outcome:** By the end, you will match the consequences of a mistake to an appropriate checking level.

**Before you begin:** For red tasks, use an authoritative source or appropriately qualified human. A person who can see information is not automatically qualified to interpret it.

## The lesson

Do not ask whether AI is trustworthy in general. Ask how much trust this answer needs for this purpose, now.

Most everyday uses are low stakes. The skill is noticing the smaller number where an error matters.

Checking should rise with both the chance of error and the harm it could cause.

### Green: good enough

Use one everyday AI answer when a mistake would cost little or nothing, such as understanding a social photograph, meme or book-cover mood.

### Amber: want it right

Use an independent source or second model when you are learning, may repeat the information or would be embarrassed by an avoidable error.

### Red: need it right

Use a qualified person, authoritative source or human visual-assistance service when an error could affect health, medication, food safety, money, safety, identity, an official decision or professional reputation.

## Try it

Classify three tasks as green, amber or red. State what could happen if each answer were wrong and what checking is proportionate.

## Checkpoint

Your checking effort increases with the consequences, and you can explain why.
