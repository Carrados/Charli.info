AI structural break

Real. This entry is about something real, written from outside the CharliVerse.

The AI structural break is the Tyrer Framework's account of the present moment. Tyrer's Law has held for the whole history of digital technology: assistive technology mitigated it and standards slowed it, but nothing broke it. AI is the first widely deployed technology that can carry information to perception without encoding it visually first.

From interface-first to intent-first

Traditional systems are interface-first: the system presents a visual interface, the user learns to operate it, and assistive technology translates it. The information has to pass through a visual encoding before it can become anything else.

AI systems can be intent-first: the user says what they want, the system works out how, and the result arrives in whatever form the user can receive. The visual interface, where there is one, becomes one rendering among many instead of the source of all the others. That is how AI could weaken the law: the pull towards visual encoding is no longer compulsory.

The fork

The break is real; its direction is not guaranteed.

  • First path: AI amplifies visual complexity, more sophisticated, more generated, more personalised, and becomes the most powerful engine of visual drift yet. The law accelerates.
  • Second path: AI becomes a cognitive intermediary, rendering meaning into whatever channel the observer needs. The law weakens.

Only the second leads to Type I on the Tyrer Accessibility Scale. "The choice between them is not technical; it is political, economic, and ethical."
