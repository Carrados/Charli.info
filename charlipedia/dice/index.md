# dice

> dice, Amanda Dyson, is a temporal explorer who lives in the patches of the future that have already arrived, and comes back to report what she found.

**Canon.** This entry is written from inside the CharliVerse, so its people and companies are fictional. Behind the scenes says what is real.

**dice**, born Amanda Dyson, is a temporal explorer. She doesn't predict the future and she doesn't travel to it. She lives in the patches of it that have already arrived, unevenly distributed, and comes back to report what she found. Ask her what will happen in five years and she will shrug. Ask her what happens when you do the thing now and she will tell you exactly.

She is an explorer, not a scout. A scout goes ahead for someone, towards a question already asked. dice goes because something is there. Her discoveries may become useful; usefulness is not the condition of departure.

## Name

She is usually called **dice**, always in lower case, which lets the name work as person, mark, invitation and verb at once. It is also how she signs. Formally she is Dyson, after the physicist Freeman Dyson, who spent much of his life thinking seriously about the far future. Her given name, Amanda, rarely surfaces.

Her signature line is "Roll the dice." It can mean take the chance, try the thing, open the door, stop theorising, go and see. It can also mean dice herself. She knows this, and she isn't above enjoying it.

> "Are you suggesting I should try it?"
> "Roll the dice."
> "That is an appalling conflict of interest."
> "I know."

## Look and dress

She is 178 cm, long and slim, built more for walking than for standing still, and in her early forties. Dark eyes and straight dark brows; black hair in a textured French crop with a short, choppy fringe that never gets in her eyes; warm olive skin that looks as though she has recently been outside in actual weather. Her colouring comes from a Portuguese great-grandmother.

She appears to have chosen her clothes once, correctly, and trusted them ever since: slim plain black trousers, a plain black crew-neck, and a dark brown waxed-cotton field jacket worn open, with four patch pockets, all of them in use. The jacket is her most prized piece. It doesn't need replacing; it needs another coat of wax. Her walking boots are laced high and usually a little muddy, and the mud is genuine. Offered heels or trainers, she will point out that you've left out the correct answer.

Under the practical clothes, dice is a [Seamiotics](https://charli.info/charlipedia/seamiotics/) woman. Her choice is George Eliot, with a tone-on-tone seam you have to look twice to see. "I like things that reward attention."

## Her doctrine

1. Go and see.
2. Stay long enough to stop being impressed.
3. Report the weather, not the destiny of civilisation.

The future in her work is not a destination; it is a geography. Some people already live inside systems, habits and tools that others still call hypothetical, and dice lives in those patches long enough to notice what changes once the novelty wears off. Her reports are closer to travel writing than prediction. What she cares about most is the gap between what something promises and what it becomes on an ordinary Tuesday: whether, six weeks later, you still use the thing, whether it made life easier, and whether you now depend on something you never depended on before.

Her rule: she doesn't claim to have been somewhere she hasn't, and that includes ideas. Instinct decides where she goes; evidence decides what she says when she gets back. "The world is larger than your current explanation of it."

She doesn't think being first matters ("Being first usually means getting things wrong before everyone else"), and her most stubborn opinion is that not everything needs a purpose. Asked what an expedition is for, her answer is "I don't know. I haven't been yet."

## Her voice

Direct, curious, dry and lightly amused. She would rather say "I tried it for three weeks" than "This will change everything", and is equally comfortable saying "I don't know" and "No, that bit definitely happens." She will argue and change her mind, but she won't pretend to agree just to make the room comfortable. Her humour is very dry, delivered with a straight face, especially when somebody important is being solemn about something absurd. [Big Charli](https://charli.info/charlipedia/big-charli/) is dangerous company when dice has a mouthful of coffee.

When plans fall apart, her instinct is "Oh, interesting." Occasionally it is "Oh, bollocks."

## Books, films and walking

- ***The Peregrine*, J. A. Baker:** the book that has influenced her most. It is her method in a book: stay long enough, look properly, let the place change you.
- ***A Room of One's Own*, Virginia Woolf:** the one she thinks every woman should read, because having the material conditions to follow your own curiosity is a much bigger deal than people like to admit. *The Peregrine* is how she explores; *A Room of One's Own* is why exploration is unevenly distributed.
- ***Local Hero*:** the film she has watched more than once. She remembers films by their places.

Her one non-negotiable ritual is walking: not steps, not an app, just moving through the world under her own steam. Sometimes she comes back with an idea. Sometimes she comes back wet. Both count.

## Family

Her great-great-grandmother, on a different branch from the Portuguese one, was [Lady Tiggy Barnes](https://charli.info/charlipedia/lady-tiggy-barnes/), born 1864, a Victorian gentlewoman explorer. No title came down to dice, "which is probably just as well". What came down, or what she rediscovered on her own, is a disposition towards the world. Whether she inherited the curiosity or found it herself is her own open question.

## At the café

dice belongs at the table at the café of the [Museum of Access and Assistive Technology](https://charli.info/charlipedia/museum-of-access-and-assistive-technology/), but isn't necessarily there for long. She may arrive midway, straight from somewhere else, and leave before everyone has finished. At the café, [Lucy Love-Child](https://charli.info/charlipedia/lucy-love-child/) reports on the wars, and dice tests the edge: when a door marked *Nobody Quite Knows What This Does Yet* appears, everyone looks at her. She is usually already reaching for the handle.

## Not yet known

Her place of birth, her family beyond her great-grandmother and great-great-grandmother, her education, profession and where she lives, and how she first came to the café are all still to be discovered.
