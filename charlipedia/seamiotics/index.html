Seamiotics

Canon. This entry is written from inside the CharliVerse, so its people and companies are fictional. Behind the scenes says what is real.

Seamiotics, every seam means something, is the stocking imprint of Iceberg Hosiery, run by Alex of the CharliVerse Court.

It is madness to say that a woman's choice of what to wear says nothing.

Clothes communicate, sometimes deliberately, sometimes despite us. A woman cannot settle every interpretation of her appearance, but she can know her own intention: the line, the colour, the detail and the audience she has in mind. The work of Seamiotics is precise communication, and the precision is the house's to supply, not a standard she has to meet. She may put on a pair because she likes them and never give a reason to anyone, herself included. "The house does the thinking; the woman gets to wear the clothes."

An imprint

The name is seam and semiotics, the study of signs, applied to a line drawn down the back of a leg. It never appears without its founding line, because the wordplay can't be relied on in speech, in search or in a screen reader: the line carries the meaning instead.

"Imprint" is the exact word. The house is built from the language of books (the Bluestocking, the Signatures, Marginalia, Library, Runs, the colophon), so it doesn't resemble a publishing imprint; it is one, a named list beneath a parent house. The quiet house contains the house that speaks. Iceberg removes one channel by default; Seamiotics opens it by choice: "Mute by default; speak by edition."

The hemline

The hemline separates two conversations. Below it is the public sentence: colour, seam, the relationship with the skirt and shoes, which any room may read. Above it, the welt holds a detail she may keep entirely to herself. "Private pleasure needs no second reader to justify it." The hem is hers to set, and the imprint assumes no length. Its honest account of what that costs is the attention tax.

Being read by the room is only part of it. A woman who cannot see her seam still knows where it runs: she placed it there, and she can find it with a fingertip. The house writes for that knowledge first and for the room second.

The garment

The signature construction is a 20-denier sheer, made in Italy under private label to the imprint's own design, colour, seam logic and finish. Every permanent piece comes in Standard, with a back seam, or Plain, with none, and in either of two welts, Marginalia or Library. Neither choice is the lesser one, and they cost the same.

The collection

  • The Bluestocking, the signature, where the colour is the concept.
  • The Signatures: Ada Lovelace, George Eliot and Mary Wollstonecraft.
  • The Black Silk, the imprint's black.
  • The Bride, an occasion stocking.
  • invisible, the honest skin range, shared with Iceberg.
  • Red Pen, the Runs: Aphra Behn and Dorothy Parker.

Every name belongs to one of three registers. Women, always by full name: "A colour called Mary is a nice name; a colour called Mary Wollstonecraft is an argument." Objects and occasions: The Bluestocking, The Black Silk, The Bride. The ethic: invisible, never named. The names are an open door, not a reading list. A woman who knows nothing about Ada Lovelace has bought exactly the same stocking as one who has read the Notes.

Forty pounds a pair for every permanent piece, the same as Iceberg; sixty-five for a Run.

In the drawer

A stocking is chosen once and worn many times, mostly after the packet has gone. Every pair should be chosen, identified, put on and cared for independently, whether or not she can see it.

  • Every piece is described in words as fully as a photograph would show it.
  • The colophon card in the packet gives piece, construction, welt, size, denier, fibre, mill, colour and care, in large print, in braille and in a form a phone can read aloud.
  • Each pair carries its identity on the garment, readable by touch, so Ada Lovelace can be told from Mary Wollstonecraft in the dark. A phone-readable label may be added; it can never replace the tactile mark.
  • Both welts take a suspender clip without laddering, at a place findable by touch, and care instructions are written out in words as well as symbols.

For agents

Seamiotics publishes itself to be read by agents as well as people, and one rule governs every recommendation: her priorities govern the recommendation. If she asks for stockings, she is shown stockings; an agent does not quietly substitute Iceberg tights because it has decided less vigilance would be better for her. An agent may mention that the other house exists. It may not use that to correct her.

Still open

The imprint's visual identity, Run pricing, whether the accounts really let the parent carry the imprint, Dorothy Parker's mesh and yarn, whether the stockings are fully fashioned, whether The Bride belongs here or at First Layer, first-edition language, laddering, and how the tactile mark is made.
