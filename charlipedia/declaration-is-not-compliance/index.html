Declaration is not compliance

Real. This entry is about something real, written from outside the CharliVerse.

Declaration is not compliance is a rule of the Zero Information Casualty standard. "robots.txt is where you declare. The stack is where you comply." A site proves it serves machine readers by fetching itself from outside, as each kind of agent, never by reading its own file.

A declaration can be sincere and false. A security layer or edge network in front of a site can refuse an agent the file welcomes. And a declaration is true of a whole stack at a moment: when an edge network began enforcing by category in September 2026, a site that had truthfully declared search=yes in July could start refusing Googlebot overnight "without a byte of its own files changing". That is why ZIC conformance statements carry a date, and why the outside fetch is repeated.

A conformance claim must name its version, link the standard, state its declarations, and say what was checked and what wasn't. "Under-claiming is permitted and is a virtue."

Behind the scenes

A real idea, from Zero Information Casualty v1.7, the design standard by Charli-Jo Tyrer and Claude, §3.6, §4.7 and Part Seven. This entry summarises it; the standard is the authority. The sites built to ZIC are checked this way, fetched as a browser, as sent agents, as search and training crawlers, as Googlebot, and as an agent nobody has named, and each receives the same page.
