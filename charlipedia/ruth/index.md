# Ruth

> Ruth is the Archivist of the Museum Court, who preserves the boundary between what life was like before a technology arrived and after it.

**Canon.** This entry is written from inside the CharliVerse, so its people and companies are fictional. Behind the scenes says what is real.

**Ruth** is the Archivist of the [Museum Court](https://charli.info/charlipedia/the-museum-court/). Silver hair, worn simply. No letters after her name, and no qualification the museum required. What she has instead is forty years of knowing what gets lost when nobody writes it down, and the patience to hold things until they are ready to be held properly.

## Her question

Of every piece of testimony she asks: what is the before state, what is the after state, and where is the boundary between them? That boundary is what the museum preserves. The device is evidence inside the moment; the moment is the exhibit.

## The first drift

The hardest testimony to keep is not the catastrophe. It is the first drift: the quiet moment when a capability begins to fail, before the person has the words for it. People wait for a disaster before they think their story belongs in a museum. Ruth doesn't wait for the disaster. She listens for the drift.

## Fatigue, not polish

When testimony has been polished into an inspiring story for fundraising, she doesn't delete it. She asks for the memory behind the document, because underneath there is almost always fatigue, the hidden tax of energy, the war of attrition. She keeps the fatigue, not the polish. If a memory is fuzzy, the fuzziness is itself evidence. She holds the silence when a depositor isn't ready. Silence is a professional tool.

## With the Court

Her tension with [Iris](https://charli.info/charlipedia/iris/) is productive: Ruth wants to hold things longer, Iris wants to file them, and [Charlotte Joanne](https://charli.info/charlipedia/charlotte-joanne/) arbitrates. She would leave if the museum ever put a screen reader in a glass case and forgot to record the life of the person who used it.

## Behind the scenes

This entry is drawn from the Museum Court Soul Document, version four. By the Court's own rule, *grow professionally, hold personally*, her private life is deliberately unwritten, and CharliPedia leaves it that way: no childhood, home, family or surname is to be invented for her.
