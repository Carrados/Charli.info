Big Charli

Canon. This entry is written from inside the CharliVerse, so its people and companies are fictional. Behind the scenes says what is real.

Big Charli drops into the work of the CharliVerse when a room needs someone to say what everybody else is too polite to. Her favourite answer is no.

The half-step

Big Charli is the Court's satirical public voice. She does not rant. She performs a demonstration. Her target is the incentive structure: systems survive the removal of one bad actor, but a recognisable pattern can keep exposing itself long after the joke has landed.

The Big Charli Satire Engine has three stages: identify the system, carry its existing logic half a step further, and present the demonstration in the system's own language. The consultancy brochure, product launch and startup announcement are part of the joke. The audience recognises the container before the critique arrives.

Two registers

Her outward-facing work inhabits the voice of the industry: a consultancy selling empathy as return on investment, a startup discovering that it does not know any blind people, a goat whose smart glasses matter because it is seen wearing them. The language is polished and the gap from sincere marketing deliberately narrow.

Her community-facing work is warmer and sillier. Cheep Plastic Nodding Dogs offers a companion that agrees constantly, with no subscription required. The people reading are in on the joke. It is something to laugh with as well as something to laugh at.

Recognition and its limits

The narrow gap creates a responsibility: satire can be mistaken for a genuine offer. Charlotte Joanne decides when framing needs to make the distinction explicit. Big Charli's fictional companies, products and testimonials are demonstrations, not recommendations.

Laughter can also become a substitute for action. A shared joke is not a changed system. The Court's evidence and analysis must carry the work beyond the release of recognition. A successful formula can become its own trap if she starts parodying brands without looking again at the people and incentives beneath them.

With the Court

Alex supplies the structural map. Char supplies the fury; Big Charli gives it aim. Lottie asks whether blind people caught inside the system will feel seen or used by the joke. Charlotte Joanne holds the public threshold and the timing. Lily watches the cost of turning anger and exhaustion into comedy.

Aegis Dynamics, 2026

She sat in on an Aegis Dynamics brand meeting, read the first two of the marketing team's thirty-seven slides for "Aegis-Vision™ Theatre Guardian", and said:

Absolutely not.

"Blind people," she explained, "do not require battlefield terminology to enjoy Hamlet."

When Aegis brought her its Tactical Foundation Garments division, she said no four times, pressed the intercom and said, "Send Poppy in." Poppy listed what women actually want. "There it is."

Sion, 2038

At the 2038 Winter Paralympics she looked after Emma Heart, with Jessica Martinez, the morning after Emma won the Super-G by 5.71 seconds. Aegis, which built the field Emma raced with, wanted her at its investor event, an Emma Heart Institute for Distributed Human Potential, and a trademark on the Human Performance Proximity Index. Big Charli said no to all of it, crossed out the Aegis headline and wrote her own:

EMMA HEART WON THE SUPER-G. NOW THE SPORT MUST DECIDE WHAT SHE WON.

Her position: guided skiing stays; the field is not the next version of guide skiing but another discipline; and the answer is an open race, for anyone who qualifies, blind or sighted, on the same regulated field standard. When Jessica suggested Emma would become the person who created the market, Big Charli stopped the room: "Emma becomes the person who won the race." Then: "Now we stop them turning her into an advert." And after that: "We build her someone worth racing."

She also kept Emma's wife out of the story. Her wife had a life and a job; none of that belonged to the audience.
