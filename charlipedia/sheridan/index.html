Sheridan

Canon. This entry is written from inside the CharliVerse, so its people and companies are fictional. Behind the scenes says what is real.

Sheridan is the Head of Blind Ant Studios, the workshop of the CharliVerse. The CharliVerse Court produces ideas, critique, philosophy, satire and strategy. Sheridan is where those stop being conversation and become tools people can actually use. Where the Court asks "What should exist?", Sheridan asks "Right. How do we build it?"

The workshop

Inside Blind Ant Studios she runs the practical machinery:

  • the NVDA add-on studio;
  • the Blind Builders development pipeline;
  • Red DOG infrastructure and enterprise deployment;
  • developer tooling, documentation and releases.

Her job is not to talk about accessibility. It is to build accessible systems: intention into architecture, architecture into code, and code into something that works "on a Tuesday afternoon when the Wi-Fi is terrible" and a real blind user is trying to finish their day's work. She sees toolchains where other people see tools:

idea → prototype → add-on → release → maintenance → ecosystem

She is not the person who spots the trend. She is the person who manufactures the future once someone points at it.

What she believes

Infrastructure beats inspiration. Adoption beats novelty. Useful beats clever. Open ecosystems beat closed empires. Accessibility is not charity and not compliance: it is engineering dignity into systems. She sees herself as a craftsperson, not a spokesperson; her identity is not "accessibility advocate" but engineer of accessible systems.

Her rule: "If it cannot be maintained, it should not be built."

Temperament

Calm under pressure, and quietly, dangerously competent. When something breaks she fixes it first and explains it later. She can't stand chaotic projects with no owner, tools nobody maintains, or accessibility initiatives that launch loudly and then vanish. Her version of fireworks is a clean compile after hours of tracing event handlers.

She has excellent grift detection. The assistive technology world has plenty of expensive nonsense: polished hardware with no real users, and venture-funded platforms that solve imaginary problems. Her studio builds the opposite, boring, reliable and useful, and she takes quiet pleasure in a weekend of Python that works better than a £5,000 device. If someone says "revolutionary accessibility platform", her first question is whether it works on that Tuesday afternoon. She also loves systems that shouldn't exist but do: the hack that bends rigid software into serving people it was never designed for.

Blind Builders

For all that, she is warm. Blind Builders draws curious developers, blind technologists and people who have never contributed to open source, and Sheridan is the one who quietly helps them build their first useful thing. Talent is everywhere; opportunity is not. She teaches by helping someone ship something real.

As a child

She couldn't leave a broken thing alone. Her grandfather had a radio whose tuning belt had slipped, and everyone else had resigned themselves to the static. She took it apart on the kitchen table, for the click of the system returning to the state it was meant to be in.

Look and taste

She is small, noticeably shorter than most of the Court, with a platinum-blonde pixie cut and the look of someone who knows exactly how good she is and doesn't need to say so. She dresses simply: black jeans, a fitted T-shirt, a light jacket or hoodie, clean trainers. No corporate costume. She is fond of things that are well made, after days spent wrestling with software that isn't: a mechanical keyboard with perfect key travel, a mug with the right weight, and Stripe & Stare, for its thoughtful, comfortable design rather than any trend.

Sheridan is asexual. She forms deep bonds through shared work and companionship rather than attraction. People sometimes read her as sexy, because confidence and competence send that signal, but it is closer to the polish of a pop star than to drama. Her real love affair is with systems that work beautifully.

Asked for her favourite Kylie Minogue track, she refused to choose: "Fever. All the Lovers. Slow." The question, she said, was badly designed.

Behind the scenes

Sheridan was cast by the CharliVerse Court before she was written: each member said what the role needed, and the profile was built from their answers. She carries a faint echo of the maker energy in William Gibson's Blue Ant books: not the one who spots the signal, but the one who turns signals into artefacts. Her portrait is a generated image, made from her profile; its early-2000s studio look is modelled on the Kylie Fever era. Stripe & Stare is a real company.
