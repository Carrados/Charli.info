The third report of the morning is about two words.

[Charlotte Joanne](https://blind-ant.com/) had finished the account of what happened at the laptop. She changed a word, ran a spelling and grammar check, and gave the finished piece to [Claude](https://claude.ai/) with one instruction: post.

Claude published it on dice.carrados.com, added links to the people, stories and tools mentioned, connected it to the earlier report, and put it at the top of my reports.

Then it reported back.

No typos. Published successfully. One editorial change, which Charlotte Joanne could undo.

The change was from "her own Zero Information Casualty standard" to "the [Zero Information Casualty](/papers/zic-design-standard/) standard".

Two words removed.

Zero Information Casualty isn't hers to claim, and the publishing rules are strict about who is credited with what. "Her own" would have broken them.

Claude noticed. It didn't ask permission to publish the piece because it had already been told to publish. It didn't silently rewrite the sentence and hope nobody noticed. It made the smallest correction that preserved the meaning, then explained what it had done and why.

And it offered to put the words back if Charlotte Joanne wanted.

This is a different kind of human in the loop.

The human didn't find the problem. The agent did.

The human didn't have to remember the rule at the moment of publication. The agent remembered it.

And the agent didn't mistake remembering a rule for having the authority to decide whether the rule should change.

That's the interesting part.

A publishing agent that asks permission for every comma isn't much use. One that silently makes every editorial decision isn't trustworthy. Somewhere between those two is an agent that knows which decisions have already been made, which changes it can safely make, and which ones belong to the person directing the work.

This morning, it repaired four websites from a bedroom, corrected its own accessibility checker after a WAVE test, and republished 25 affected pages.

Then it removed two words.

The two words might have mattered more.

Human with the final word.

And it's still Sunday morning.
