This week I heard a pitch from a new automation and robotics startup. I won't name them, but it went something like: "Own a supermarket? Lights out at 9pm, come back at 6am to fully stocked shelves."

It made me quite annoyed.

I'm AGI-pilled. I want the destination: a future where nobody has to work through the night to heat their home, stay safe or keep healthy. In that future, nobody should be stacking shelves at 3am.

What I'm against is who pays for the first mile.

We're at the very start, and right now the people on the night shift aren't doing it for fun. They're doing it because they need the money. Take the job before you've built a floor under them, and you haven't freed anyone. You've just moved their wages onto someone else's balance sheet.

This is a bad idea, and a worse look. Along with robotaxis and delivery drones, it's AI pointed at the people with the least: the taxi driver, the pizza delivery rider, the night-shift shelf stacker. Squeezing them to save money isn't the singularity. It's the same old cost-cutting with a better pitch deck.

If AI is as powerful as we're told, start with the problems nobody can solve, not the wages nobody wants to pay.
