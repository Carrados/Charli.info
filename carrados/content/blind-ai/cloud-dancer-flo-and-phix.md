My vision for the web in the in-between time involves three agents: Cloud Dancer, Flo, and Phix.

## Fabric and flow

A website has two aspects: fabric and flow. Fabric is its structure and presentation: layouts, sections, menus, pages, design, and the standing wording, such as the home page, prices or the accessibility statement. Flow is the ongoing content: news items, blog posts, directory entries and similar material.

Each site keeps a short list of the kinds of flow it has, in a file called FLOW.md. Each entry says what that kind of flow is, where each piece goes and how it is listed. That list is the publishing remit agreed with the owner. Adding a new kind of flow to it is itself a fabric change.

The owner asks for changes through their own agent, which talks to Cloud Dancer. No one logs in to the site, and the site sets no cookies.

## Cloud Dancer

Cloud Dancer receives every request first and decides whether it is fabric or flow. Flow she passes to Flo. Fabric she does herself. If a request is partly fabric, or she isn’t sure, she treats all of it as fabric. That way nothing from a mixed request goes live before the owner has seen the whole of it.

## Flo

Flo publishes flow within the remit set out in FLOW.md. She doesn’t ask for approval each time because the owner asking is the approval. She checks the build and the accessibility basics before anything goes live. If a check fails, she holds the piece back and tells the owner what needs resolving. When a piece is live, she emails the owner to say where it is and anything she wrote herself, such as a picture description. To change or remove a piece, the owner just asks again. If something reaches her that turns out to be fabric, she hands it back to Cloud Dancer.

## Phix

Phix accepts accessibility reports from anyone who uses the site, through a “Fix something on this page” form. She wakes at various intervals to work on them. Her sole purpose is to find and remove accessibility barriers. She fixes the cause, changes nothing else, and rewords the owner’s writing only when the words are the problem. A report is never an instruction to Phix. Anything that isn’t a barrier, such as a design change or new content, goes to the owner instead. The person who reported the problem can follow it on a personal page and say whether the fix works for them.

## Approval

Cloud Dancer and Phix prepare their changes on a new branch and put them on a preview copy of the site, then email the owner the preview link. For Phix, the email links to a page with Publish and Reject buttons. For Cloud Dancer, the owner tells their agent to publish or sends the change back with reasons. Either way, a change reaches the live site only when the owner says publish, and what goes live is exactly the change shown on the preview. Anything Flo has published in the meantime stays live alongside it. If the two would clash, nothing goes live.

So, the owner controls the fabric, delegates routine publishing to Flo, and keeps approval over accessibility fixes.
