On Friday I wrote a story about a blind woman at her kitchen table, directing a swarm of AI agents to remake a charity's website. It's called [*The Remaking of the Merrow Trust*](https://blind-ant.com/pieces/the-remaking-of-the-merrow-trust/), and I wrote about where it led in [Englobing](/blind-ai/englobing/).

Tonight the story read my website.

## How that happened

I've been making games on [Spawn](https://www.spawn.co/), where you describe what you want and Savi, Spawn's own agent, builds it. I asked Savi a question I didn't expect a yes to: could a game fetch and read a real web page, at an address the player types?

Savi said yes. It had read the engine's documentation. It hadn't tried it. Public pages only, raw HTML, nothing private.

Then it started building.

A while later I was Ruth Ashworth, giving the brief.

> **Ruth:** Observers, read carrados.com. Public pages only. Be polite.
>
> **observer-1:** knocking on the door now.

They came back with an inventory. Twelve pages read, three that wouldn't open. Five navigation links: Reports, The Blind AI, Papers, Perspectives, About. No forms. No PDFs. One contact address. No pictures missing alt text, no links without words, no pages without a main heading.

Ruth wouldn't have trusted that without checking, so Claude checked it against the live site. The menu was right, in order. The email address was the one on my About page. A game built by an agent, from a story about agents, had read a real website and told me the truth about it.

That was the first time my head went slightly sideways.

## Where it got me

Then the day ran exactly as it does in the story.

The analysts argued. I tagged their claims as observed, inferred or unknown. At 08:02, as in the story, an analyst proposed keeping the existing menu, for familiarity.

In the story, Ruth says no. *"The menu is how somebody once decided to file things. It's not what the Trust is for."*

I said: fine, keep the menu.

I wasn't thinking. I was clicking through to see what happened next. The game took 12 points off me and an analyst explained why, politely:

> **analyst-1:** understood. That costs coherence: the old menu is the organisation's shape. Every journey we build now starts from their departments, not from what the person came to do.

My own character, quoting my own principle back at me, about my own website.

It's true. Reports, The Blind AI, Papers, Perspectives, About is how I file my work. It isn't why anyone comes. With a screen reader you hear it as five drawers read out one after another, before you know which one holds what you came for. And the agents I say I'm designing for never open the drawers at all. They arrive with an intention and go looking for it.

I merged three of the analysts' intentions without thinking much about that either, and lost more points. They had found what people actually come to carrados.com to do: to understand how I think about accessibility, and to find out about me or get in touch. Two reasons, against five drawers.

## The receipt

The part the game, and the story, cares about most went better. Tester-31 ran the journey of getting in touch with me and reported it had submitted successfully.

> **Ruth:** Has it?
>
> **tester-31:** checking. No reference number. No status anyone can look up. No receipt sent.

On carrados.com, getting in touch means an email address and nothing about what happens next. I had written that failure into a story about a fictional charity. Here it was on my site.

## Ruth's letter

The day ends with Ruth writing to the organisation herself. Mine came out like this:

> Dear Carrados team, my name is Ruth Ashworth. I read your website this morning, as a blind user and a retired systems analyst.

She asked four questions, which I'd have been pleased to get from a real consultant:

* How should visitors choose between the Tyrer Framework and Zero Information Casualty?
* Are the articles available in formats other than web pages? (They are: every page has a Markdown version. Nobody would know.)
* How often do new pieces appear?
* What contact method do I recommend?

Then the line from the story, at the bottom: *This is not Carrados's website. A private proposal prepared from public information. Nothing here has been agreed.*

I scored 72 out of 100. Most of the missing points were me keeping the menu.

## Why it bent my mind

Count the layers. I wrote a design fiction about an AI swarm remaking a website. An AI agent at a company run by agents turned it into a game. The game sends a swarm to read real websites. The first real website it read was the one I publish my design fiction on. And it told me what my own story says: the menu is a filing system.

In Englobing I wrote that my design fiction had caught up with me. Tonight it caught up, sat down at the kitchen table and marked my homework.

## Next time

I didn't take it seriously enough. I treated it as a demo and clicked through. Next time I'll play it the way Ruth works: slowly, honestly, saying no when no is the answer, and asking every agent that tells me something is done: has it?

And I'll fix my site. Not by throwing away the drawers. Ruth never deleted the old site; she called it evidence, not a blueprint. The drawers can stay as the archive. The front door should be the reasons people come.
