# Christmas With Love: review against the Sloppy Jo vision

Reviewed by Claude on Thursday 5 November 2026. The picture is the 4 November render: `master.png` in Charli.info, `blind-ant/studio/cards/designs/christmas-with-love/`. I looked at the whole picture and at four close-up crops (the flames, the wax, and the holly on each side). This review is judged against Sloppy Jo's intentions, not against how photographic it looks.

## What the picture shows

Two ivory pillar candles stand side by side on a dark, glossy wooden table, the left one taller. Each has a cream teardrop flame with a thin orange halo. Small gold, cream and salmon-pink discs of blurred light fill the darkness behind, from the top edge down to just above the flames. A loose ring of dark holly leaves and red berry clusters lies around the candles' feet. One bronze-gold bauble is at the front left, half hidden by holly, and a smaller one is alone at the far right.

**Alt text (unchanged and still true):** Two lit ivory candles glow side by side on a dark wooden table, among glossy holly leaves, clusters of red berries and two small gold baubles, with soft golden lights blurred in the darkness behind them.

## Verdict

**Competent, not yet convincing.** The idea and the silhouette are sound. The finish, the light and the colour discipline are not yet at Sloppy Jo's standard. In Sloppy Jo's words, don't call it finished because it is competent.

## The seven principles

1. **Design before realism: passes.** At thumbnail size, the two white columns with their flames read instantly. The flames are stylised teardrops rather than real fire, which is the right choice.
2. **Elegant geometry: partly passes.** The candles and flames are clean, simple forms. But:
   - The candle tops are flat lids with a hard edge, with no softened rim or melt pool, so they read as plastic.
   - The holly is a scattered ring that's cut off at the bottom of the picture.
   - In the right-hand cluster, bare stalks poke up like twigs.
   - Several things could be removed without loss (Sloppy Jo's check), so this doesn't fully pass.
3. **Rich materials: fails.** This is the weakest point.
   - The wax fades from bright cream at the top to a muddy amber-brown band halfway down, most strongly on the right candle. It reads as staining, not as light passing through wax: a defect, not stylisation.
   - The left candle has a soft diagonal pale wedge across its middle that looks like a smear.
   - The baubles are dull bronze with one hard highlight each, not gold.
   - The holly leaves are so dark that their gloss barely shows.
   - The berries are good: glossy, with clean highlights.
4. **Expressive lighting: fails.** The flames glow but light almost nothing:
   - The tops of the candles don't catch the flames.
   - There are no warm pools on the table.
   - The holly and baubles sit in near darkness, so the bottom third is murky.
   - Nothing separates the candles from the background, such as a rim of light.
   - It isn't theatrical; it's under-lit.
5. **Deliberate colour: fails the check.** The closest one-sentence palette is "ivory and gold candlelight on brown-black, with holly green and berry red", but the salmon-pink discs in the background are stray hues, and the brown in the wax is another. Two greens appear as well: a bluish green on the left leaves and a darker green on the right.
6. **Compositional discipline: partly passes.**
   - The focal point is clear: the two flames, slightly left of centre, with the taller one leading. The top third is calm enough for the headline.
   - The blurred lights are spread evenly and at random, so nothing guides the eye. They also come down level with the left flame and compete with it.
   - The holly ring is cut off at the bottom edge.
   - The far-right bauble floats alone, doing no work.
   - The band where the table meets the darkness is an empty strip across the middle.
7. **Exceptional finish: partly passes.** The edges are clean and the noise is gone. But the brown band on the wax is a rough tonal transition, and at phone size (about 6 cm wide) the bottom third goes to mud. Holly, baubles and berries merge into dark shapes with red dots.

## The five tests

- **Does it please?** Yes, at a glance: candlelight and twinkly blur are pleasant.
- **Does it inform?** Yes: candles, holly, Christmas, warmth. It matches its description.
- **Does it fit the fiction?** Only partly. The brief for this card is "romantic, with richer colours". It's pleasant but generic, closer to a stock photo than to something that "knows what it is". Nothing in it says "love".
- **Does it impress?** Not yet. Nothing in it is exquisitely made; the wax and the lighting give it away.
- **Does it linger?** To be judged across all seven cards. On its own it's forgettable.

## Defects and deliberate stylisation

Defects, to fix:
- the brown band on the wax
- the smear on the left candle
- flat candle tops
- bronze baubles
- holly lost in the dark
- stray pink in the background
- the twig-like stalks
- the holly cut off at the bottom

Deliberate stylisation, to keep:
- teardrop flames
- the even, glossy berries
- the strong blur of the background
- the dark, calm top third

## What succeeds

The concept is simple and strong: two flames, side by side. The silhouette works at any size, and the calm top third does its job for the headline.

## What I'd change, in order

1. Light the scene from the flames: warm pools on the table and holly, the candle tops catching the light, and a faint rim to separate the candles from the background.
2. Remake the wax as luminous alabaster that glows from within near the top and stays ivory all the way down, never brown.
3. Soften the candle tops with a rounded rim and a shallow lit pool.
4. Fix the palette in one sentence before touching anything else, and remove the pink from the background.
5. Rebuild the holly as a deliberate crescent that sits wholly in the frame, with visible gloss and no bare stalks. Use one bauble, placed on purpose and truly gold.
6. Put the blurred lights where they help: denser behind the headline, thinning towards the flames, so they frame rather than compete.
7. Then ask the question the five tests leave open: what in the picture says love?
