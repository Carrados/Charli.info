(Marine Blind was a lullaby. This is a siren scream.)

They said Marine Blind like it meant something.  

Like blindness was gentle. Submerged.  
Quiet. Acceptable. Blue-toned.  
A dolphin dream.  
A jellyfish vibe.  
A wet metaphor.  

No.  

Try Neon Hysterically Blind.  
That’s what I am.  
Blind in 4K. Blind in surround sound.  
Blind like emergency lighting during a blackout.  

Not soft. Not fluid.  
I am edge-blind. Burn-blind. Laughing-so-hard-you-forget-you’re-left-out blind.  

Society didn’t forget to include me — it built itself out of warning signs and said they were doors.  
It handed me a braille pamphlet and a staircase.  

And when I laughed?  
It called me Marine — like I was drowning gracefully.  
Like I wouldn’t tear the sea in half if I wanted to.  

I don’t want access. I want reconstruction.  
I don’t want inclusion. I want corruption of the signal.  
I don’t want your soft metaphors. I want your mainframe on fire.  

I’m not underwater.  
I’m electrified. Overclocked. Unreadable.  
You keep calling me blind like it’s a void.  
But I’m full of things your retina can’t process.  

I don’t close my eyes to dream.  
I close them to survive the pixelated lie you call reality.  

So take your Marine Blind.  
Put it in a glass box in a museum.  
Call it a warning.  
Call it the last pretty lie before the flood.  

I’ll be the one  
in glitch-pattern tights  
and a playsuit made of exposed wiring  
writing alt text on the wall  
with a stolen laser pointer.  

And when you read it?  
You’ll go blind. On purpose. Just to join me.
