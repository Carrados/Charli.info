**Niamh Rourke interviews Siobhán McDonnell, also known as Down the Banksy, ahead of the inaugural 2026 NoBell Awards in Liverpool.**

**NIAMH ROURKE:** Siobhán McDonnell, Down the Banksy, you are hosting the inaugural NoBell Awards in Liverpool. How did that happen?

**SIOBHÁN McDONNELL:** Administrative failure, mostly. Someone had the good sense to invent an award for not being a bellend, then made the obvious mistake of asking me to stand near a microphone.

**NIAMH:** You approve of the name?

**SIOBHÁN:** Of course I approve of the name. It is vulgar, local, precise and morally useful. That is almost my entire aesthetic.

**NIAMH:** For anyone outside Britain, or outside Liverpool, what does “don’t be a bell” mean?

**SIOBHÁN:** It means: don’t be a bellend. But crucially, it is not normally said like a policy paper. It is something you say to your mate before they make themselves worse.

You can say it with affection. You can say it with warning. You can say it across a pub table, outside a chippy, in a WhatsApp group, at a planning meeting, in a changing room, or to a man on the internet who has confused having a podcast with having a duty.

It is not “be perfect.” It is not “be pure.” It is not “join my side and repeat my slogans.”

It is: you know better than this, love. Don’t be a bell.

**NIAMH:** So the NoBell Awards are not really awards for niceness?

**SIOBHÁN:** No. Niceness is too soft. Plenty of terrible things arrive wearing nice shoes.

The NoBell Awards are for decency under conditions where bellendry was available, rewarded, branded, monetised, algorithmically boosted, or electorally useful.

That is the distinction.

**NIAMH:** The categories include public life, social media, accessibility, AI, meetings, romance, quiet usefulness and lifetime achievement. That is quite a range.

**SIOBHÁN:** Because bellendry is not sector-specific. It is a transferable skill.

You can be a bell in public life by mistaking cruelty for strength.  
You can be a bell on social media by turning every thought into a performance injury.  
You can be a bell in accessibility by using disabled people as moral wallpaper.  
You can be a bell in AI by becoming either a prophet or a panic merchant.  
You can be a bell in meetings by converting oxygen into agenda items.  
You can be a bell in romance by making love feel like paperwork with kissing.  
You can be a bell while doing charity, art, politics, business, activism, anything.

The NoBell Awards say: here are some people who had the chance, and declined.

**NIAMH:** That sounds almost gentle.

**SIOBHÁN:** Don’t tell anyone. I have a reputation.

**NIAMH:** The logo is a purple bell.

**SIOBHÁN:** Yes. Purple because it is ceremonial, ridiculous and slightly Catholic. A bell because subtlety is for people with funding rounds.

**NIAMH:** You’ve said before that your work is public moral address.

**SIOBHÁN:** Yes. People keep saying street artist, like I am emotionally attached to brick. I am not. Brick is where the notice fit. I issue notices.

And this is a notice too.

The NoBell Awards are basically a civic notice saying:

**OPPORTUNITY TO BE A BELLEND RECEIVED.  
OPPORTUNITY DECLINED.  
DECENCY RECORDED.**

That is beautiful paperwork.

**NIAMH:** Is there a danger that an award for “not being a bellend” becomes smug?

**SIOBHÁN:** Huge danger. Enormous. The moment you think you have permanently achieved NoBell status, you are already halfway up Bellend Hill with a packed lunch.

That is why it has to be funny. The joke protects it from sanctimony. Nobody gets to stand there glowing like a moral scented candle. The whole thing has to keep one boot in the Mersey and one eyebrow raised.

**NIAMH:** So it is not about declaring people saints?

**SIOBHÁN:** No. Saints are dangerous. They get turned into statues, then nobody listens to what they said.

The NoBell Awards are not saying these people are flawless. They are saying: in this area, at this moment, on this evidence, they did something useful without making it all about their own magnificence.

That is enough. In fact, that is plenty.

**NIAMH:** Why Liverpool?

**SIOBHÁN:** Because Liverpool understands the difference between sentiment and solidarity.

Also because “don’t be a bell” sounds better here. Some phrases need their native weather.

**NIAMH:** What do you want the ceremony to feel like?

**SIOBHÁN:** Like a civic hall got possessed by a pub argument and somehow came out kinder.

No red carpet nonsense. No tech-bro lanyard mysticism. No “inspirational journey” music under people’s trauma. Just a room full of people saying: maybe the highest available honour, in this particular century, is to have power and not immediately become unbearable.

**NIAMH:** Final line?

**SIOBHÁN:** Prestige is nice. Decency is better.

And if that sounds too polished, I’ll translate:

Don’t be a bell, la.

It’s not hard.
