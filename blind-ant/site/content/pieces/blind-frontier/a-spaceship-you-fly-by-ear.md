Well, I've gone back into space. And this time, Newton is coming with me.

**[Blind Frontier](https://www.spawn.co/@charlijo/blind-frontier)** is my latest game on Spawn, released today, 3 November 2026. It's a space trading and flight simulator set around Ross 154 and neighbouring star systems.

And I'm blind, so naturally I've made a spaceship you can fly by ear.

This isn't point-and-click space travel. The ship uses Newtonian physics. Accelerate towards your destination, turn around, fire your engines to slow down, and try not to sail straight past the space station at an embarrassing velocity.

You can fly manually, using spoken instruments, audio cues and sonar, or ask Lyra, your ship's AI, to take the controls.

There's trading, too. Buy robotics, medicine, alloys and other cargo, travel between star systems, and sell where the prices are better. Upgrade your weapons and shields, encounter pirates, and work your way towards the coveted Elite rating.

## But there's another way to play

You can give Lyra standing orders. She'll examine the markets, choose profitable cargo, refuel, fly between systems, trade and report back. You can intervene whenever you like.

I tried this myself. Lyra spent her time hauling medicine, robotics and deep pearls between Ross 154 and Lalande 21185. Before long, she'd earned me more than a million credits and the Elite rating.

Rather satisfying, although I did begin to wonder whether I was the captain or merely the one financing the expedition.

The game is also an experiment in accessibility and human-agent collaboration. What happens when you build a space simulator around sound, language and an AI copilot, rather than treating accessibility as something to bolt on afterwards?

It's an early release, and there's plenty more to build. I'd particularly welcome feedback from both blind and sighted players. Does the flight make sense? Is the interface manageable? Is it actually fun?

**Blind Frontier is free to play in your browser on Spawn.** No installation required.

Come aboard. Lyra has the flight plan.

And remember: in space, nobody can hear you overshoot the docking station.
