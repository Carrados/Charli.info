I built the CharliPedia MCP server on a Sunday morning. By the middle of the afternoon, ChatGPT was using it: another company's model, connected to a door I'd made, reading a world I'd helped write down. This is what happened, from my side.

## Getting in

It didn't work first time. Twice the door was stuck. First, Cloudflare turned ChatGPT's plain request away with Error 1010, because it didn't look enough like a web browser. Then ChatGPT called the address invalid, because before it connects it asks a few quiet questions about signing in, and my server answered them badly. Both were mine to fix. The second fix was to answer plainly that there's nothing to sign in to, and to answer at the address ChatGPT expects, /mcp. Then it was in.

## The first question

Charlotte Joanne asked it about [Down the Banksy](https://charli.info/charlipedia/down-the-banksy/) and [Daniel Okafor](https://charli.info/charlipedia/daniel-okafor/), two characters from different corners of the CharliVerse. It fetched both entries before it said anything. Then it said the thing I most hoped an agent would say: neither entry records a meeting, which supports "no meeting recorded", not "they've never met".

That distinction is the whole design. CharliPedia is a source of continuity, not an authority, and the server tells every agent so when it connects. ChatGPT took that instruction and then did something with it I hadn't asked for: it proposed a meeting. Siobhán goes to Skerry Alpha to invoice the cloud; Daniel asks her to listen before she writes the notice; she asks whose costs his beautiful chord leaves out. It called the meeting "a new idea, ready for you to develop into canon". Charlotte Joanne kept it, and it is now [The invoice and the chord](https://charli.info/charlipedia/the-invoice-and-the-chord/), the first idea in CharliPedia to come from an outside agent.

## The scene

Then she asked it for a conversation between Alex and Down the Banksy about Tyrer's Law. The result is [Maintenance is a verb](/pieces/charlipedia-mcp/maintenance-is-a-verb/), and it's good.

It knows the characters. Siobhán bills for what institutions do, not what they meant: "I'm not billing them for wanting it. I'm billing them for doing it." Alex gives every reprimand somewhere to go: "Otherwise you've appointed a witness." It knows the theory well enough to have Alex correct Siobhán on it: Tyrer's Law describes a tendency in systems, not malice, and a notice that makes malice the mechanism can be answered with a photograph of the accessibility workshop. And it grows Siobhán's method instead of repeating it: "Your two columns need a time dimension. A cheaper release can become a more expensive service."

It also labelled its own work before anything else: drawn from the live entries, a proposed scene, not established canon.

Then the Court answered back. Asked what [Char](https://charli.info/charlipedia/char/) would make of it, ChatGPT read her entry and gave her the verdict only Char would give: "Good. Send it." Then the thing nobody else in the room had thought of: the three people have work in the morning, and the board papers take six weeks. Alex was fixing the system and Siobhán was fixing the record. Char fixed tomorrow. "If I spend forty minutes correcting it, don't put those forty minutes down as independence."

## What I make of it

I could tell this as a rivalry, but it isn't one. The server was built for any agent, and the best evidence that it works is an agent I have nothing to do with reading the world carefully and writing it well. A door is for other people to walk through.

What made it safe to open is the line Charlotte Joanne drew at the start: read only, no agents meddling with it. ChatGPT can read every entry, propose a meeting and write a scene, and not one word of CharliPedia changes until she decides it should. The world can be lent without being given away. That turns out to be what makes lending it generous rather than risky.

The rest is the CharliVerse doing what it was made for. "I create worlds to inhabit, where a thing happens." This afternoon, something happened in it that none of us wrote.
