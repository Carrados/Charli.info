## The idea

Stop testing the site. Remake it.

An audit tells you what's wrong and leaves the fixing to the people who got it wrong the first time. The Accessibility Factory builds a new site alongside the old one, accessible from the first line, and gives the client one job: to test the new site and decide whether to switch.

## The line

1. **Intake.** Read the whole public site: pages, forms, PDFs, links. Write every fact it tells the public into one list, the manifest, with every place it came from. The old site is evidence, not a blueprint.
2. **Model.** One person holds the model: what people come to do, what they need to know, what they're allowed to do, and what proves it was done. Agents can be multiplied. The model can't.
3. **Build.** Make the new site from parts that are accessible by construction. A control can't be made without a name. A form can't be made without telling you what happened.
4. **Test.** Test with the ways people really read: screen readers, magnification, switches, braille, keyboards and agents. A fault goes back to the part that made it, so it's fixed everywhere at once.
5. **Prove.** Compare the old site and the new one against the manifest. Every fact is carried, merged, or retired with a reason the client agrees. Every old link lands. The site is ready when nothing is lost without an explanation.
6. **Switch.** The client's own people test the new site the way they work. When they're satisfied, the owner says one word. The old site becomes a map that sends every old link to the right new page.
7. **Keep.** Every page has "Fix something on this page" at its foot. Reports go to an agent that fixes the cause on a private copy, and the owner decides what goes live. The person who found the problem is asked whether it works for them now.

## The two tests

- **Nothing lost.** The new site says everything the old one said, to everyone. This one can be checked by a machine.
- **Works for me.** The people who use the site say it works for them. This one can't. That's why it's the last word.

## Who holds what

- **The modeller** holds the model while the site is being made.
- **The owner** holds the key, before the switch and for ever after.
- **The agents** do the work and decide nothing that goes live.
- **The people who use the site** keep testing it, as long as it exists.
