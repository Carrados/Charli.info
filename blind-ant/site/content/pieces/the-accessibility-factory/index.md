## About the Piece

For twenty-five years, accessibility has been something done to a website after it is built. Someone tests it, writes a long report of everything that's wrong, and the people who built it work through the list while building new problems. This Piece imagines the other way round: stop testing the site and remake it, accessible from the start, alongside the old one. The client doesn't fix anything. They test the new site, and when it says everything the old one said, they switch over. After that, every page carries a link, "Fix something on this page", so the people who use the site keep it right.

It brings together three Pieces. Ruth Ashworth and the Merrow Trust come from [The Remaking of the Merrow Trust](/pieces/the-remaking-of-the-merrow-trust/). The fix link comes from [Works For Me](/pieces/works-for-me/). The agents who look after the site afterwards come from [Three Agents](/pieces/three-agents/).

## Behind the fiction

- **Invented:** Ruth Ashworth, the Merrow Trust, its chair Gwen Hale, its trainer Dev Mistry and the member who sends the first report. The Accessibility Factory, as one service that remakes a whole site and switches it over, doesn't exist yet. This is what it could be.
- **Real:** the parts it is made of. The "Fix something on this page" link has been at the foot of every page on [unremarkable.me](https://unremarkable.me/) since 7 November 2026. [Phix](/pieces/the-making-of-phix/), the agent that answers it, wakes at 6 in the morning and at midday, makes each fix on a private copy of the site, and can't publish anything: only the owner can. Cloud Dancer and Flo, the other two agents in [Three Agents](/pieces/three-agents/), are real too.
- **Real:** [Zero Information Casualty](https://zeroinformationcasualty.org/), the standard these sites are built to, whose name says the same thing as the switchover test: nothing lost. The way the test uses it here is imagined.
