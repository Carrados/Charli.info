*Recovered fragment // Speculative Accessibility // Week 3*

If the Structural Break is the fork in the road, the Tyrer Scale is the map of what lies beyond it. And the most useful way to read that map is to track not the technology, but the professional whose job it is to ensure the technology serves everyone. What happens to the accessibility consultant as the civilisation ascends?

In the Type 0 world — the world we currently inhabit — that professional is a Retrofitter. Their job exists because systems are built broken and someone has to fix them afterward. WCAG pass/fail is the measure of success. The work is real and necessary and entirely symptomatic. Every audit is a bill presented to a civilisation for the cost of Tyrer’s Law.

The first transition, playing out now and accelerating through the 2030s, ends the Newtonian Web. When interfaces are generated at the moment of interaction rather than served as fixed objects, the audit of finished websites becomes meaningless — there are no finished websites to audit. The consultant’s role doesn’t disappear; it migrates upstream. The new work is governing the Generative Ceiling: ensuring that the intent-first AI systems have a Right to Inspect the source data, so that non-visual renderings don’t need to be approximated from visual ones after the fact. They can be generated natively, simultaneously, from the same semantic substrate. The Retrofitter becomes a Data Integrity Specialist. They don’t fix the interface. They fix what the interface is made of.

By mid-century, the principles that restructured the digital world begin restructuring the physical one. A blind person missing a bus because the stop failed to broadcast its identifier is not an accessibility problem. It is an infrastructure failure — the same category as a burst water main or a failed traffic signal. The consultant has become a civil engineer, managing the environmental metadata of cities: the information layer that communicates geometry, state, crowd density, arrival times, spatial relationships directly to whoever needs them, through whatever channel they use to receive the world. Accessibility is now a public utility. You don’t hire someone to provide it. You pay taxes for the layer that makes it invariant.

At the far end of the scale — Type III, the horizon the framework names but does not pretend is imminent — the concept of disability has been rendered inoperative by the civilisation’s information architecture. Not cured, not accommodated, not assisted. Inoperative. The Perceptual Engineer manages what is called the Zero Information Casualty condition: the state in which no agent fails to receive information in time to act on it, because information is rendered at the point of consciousness, not mediated through the accident of biological sensory channels. Perceptual Sovereignty — the right of every agent to full informational access to their environment — is a constitutional guarantee, as foundational as any other right the civilisation has decided to enshrine.

This is what a ZIC zone looks like in practice, rendered as design fiction from a Liverpool transit core sometime after 2100: no staff member, no specialised app, no accessible version of a visual original. The geometry of the station — crowd density, maglev velocity, the heat signature of a coffee kiosk — flows as a high-fidelity data stream, shaped to the receiver. There is no visual version being missed. Information integrity is 100%. No casualties reported.

That field report is fiction. But it is the logical terminus of a trajectory that begins with the question Rungwell is asking today — why are organisations still failing at accessibility? — and follows it all the way down to the root. The answer is Tyrer’s Law. The remedy is Information Integrity as engineering invariant. The destination is a civilisation that has made information casualties as unacceptable as leaving people without water.

The accessibility professional’s journey from Compliance Auditor to Semantic Architect to Environmental Registrar to Perceptual Engineer is not a career progression. It is a civilisational one. Each role is only possible because the previous generation held the line, named the problem accurately, and refused to accept the retrofit as the final answer.
