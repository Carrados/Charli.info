A Blind Ant design fiction. Red DOG does not exist (yet). Nothing here is a real announcement, and no real person, company or customer is quoted.

Liverpool and Brussels, 7 September 2027. FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

Red DOG today announced the launch of its enterprise support and deployment service for [NVDA](https://www.nvaccess.org/), the free and open-source screen reader. It is the first supplier approved under the European Digital Accessibility Infrastructure agency's (EDAI) framework tender.

## The problem

Large organisations tell the same story. Their blind and partially sighted staff prefer NVDA, and their IT departments hesitate to deploy it. The software is not the obstacle. Documentation, packaging, security review, a number to ring when it breaks, and a contract a procurement team can sign are the obstacles.

## What ships today

- A deployment pack for managed Windows estates, with a published security and update review.
- Three support tiers, from business-hours email to a named engineer on call.
- The NVDA Add-On Studio, a workshop for blind developers and accessibility specialists to build, test and share add-ons.

## What Red DOG owes NVDA

Red DOG works under EDAI's 40/40/20 clause. On every contract, 40% of net surplus goes to NV Access in a ring-fenced fund NV Access alone controls, 40% stays with Red DOG, and 20% goes to the Add-On Commons for the developers who build extensions. Red DOG's accounts are audited and published each year. Red DOG has no say in NVDA's roadmap, and says so in its contracts.

"A support company that takes from an open project is a parasite, and one that gives back half of what is left is a colleague. We would rather be the second," said Ines Calloway, Red DOG co-founder. (Ines Calloway is a fictional person.)

## First customer

"Our blind colleagues were already using NVDA on their own laptops. What we lacked was a way to deploy it properly and get help when it mattered," said Anneke Verhoeven, head of digital workplace at Rhein-Maas Regionalbank. (Anneke Verhoeven and Rhein-Maas Regionalbank are fictional.)

## The symbol

Red DOG's mark is a guide dog in a technology vest: independence, mobility and confidence, in a world where the terrain includes the internet.

## How to make this real

This piece is released under a Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) licence, and the idea is yours to take. A realistic order of work:

1. Ask NV Access what funding structure would help them and what would harm their independence, before anyone designs anything.
2. Write the 40/40/20 clause as a standalone procurement term that works for any open project, and test it with a procurement lawyer.
3. Find one public body or regulated business with a real deployment problem and a budget.
4. Build the deployment pack and support service for that one customer first.
5. Publish the accounts from the first day.
