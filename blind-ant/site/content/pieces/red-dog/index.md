## About the Piece

Two press releases from a Europe that does not exist yet. In the first, an agency is set up to fund open-source assistive technology as shared infrastructure. In the second, the first company approved to sell support for NVDA explains what it owes the people who make it.

Between them they test one idea, the 40/40/20 clause: what would it take for a company to earn money from a free screen reader without taking it over? The second release ends with a realistic order of work, for anyone who wants to make it real.

## Behind the fiction

The European Digital Accessibility Infrastructure agency (EDAI), Red DOG, the 40/40/20 clause and every person and customer quoted are invented. Nothing in either release is a real announcement.

Some of what they rest on is real. [NVDA](https://www.nvaccess.org/) is a free, open-source screen reader maintained by NV Access, which has not been consulted and has not endorsed this Piece. The European Accessibility Act is a real law, and the first release names the question it left open. Red Hat is a real company, named only as the releases say. Both releases are released under a Creative Commons Attribution licence (CC BY 4.0), and the idea is free to take.
