**Sloppy Jo and Down the Banksy, over coffee**

*The museum café. Two coffees, one biscuit between them, and the poster propped against the sugar bowl.*

**SIOBHÁN:** You put a United shirt in my poster.

**SLOPPY JO:** Good morning to you too.

**SIOBHÁN:** I’m a Liverpool girl. I’d handle that shirt with tongs.

**SLOPPY JO:** Look at the hand holding it. Two fingers, collar only.

**SIOBHÁN:** *(She peers at it.)* That’s good, that. Don’t tell anyone I said so.

**SLOPPY JO:** Four hours, that thumb.

**SIOBHÁN:** You call yourself Sloppy and you spent four hours on a thumb.

**SLOPPY JO:** The name is a joke. The thumb is not.

**SIOBHÁN:** Is Sloppy Jo a sandwich or a threat?

**SLOPPY JO:** The sandwich is messy. I’m not.

* * *

**SIOBHÁN:** The wall version had brick dust. A bad temper. You’ve moisturised it.

**SLOPPY JO:** I’ve given it a finish. Different thing.

**SIOBHÁN:** Put some dirt on it, then. A bit of grit. Make it look like someone cross did it at night.

**SLOPPY JO:** No.

**SIOBHÁN:** No?

**SLOPPY JO:** Dirt added to look real is a costume. It says “this was done in anger” when it was done on a Tuesday, in good light, by me. Your brick is evidence. Mine would be a prop.

**SIOBHÁN:** So you won’t fake my wall.

**SLOPPY JO:** I’d be forging your provenance. I’d rather be a very polished artist than a very polished liar.

**SIOBHÁN:** That’s a good answer. I hate it.

* * *

**SLOPPY JO:** I don’t aim for belief. I aim for conviction.

**SIOBHÁN:** Conviction. Now you’re speaking my language. We had convictions. We also had confession.

**SLOPPY JO:** It means the image knows what it is. Nobody has to ask whether it’s real.

**SIOBHÁN:** Mine isn’t asking to be believed. It’s asking whether you can look the man in the eye.

**SLOPPY JO:** Mine asks whether you’ll stop walking.

**SIOBHÁN:** Mine doesn’t care if you stop walking.

**SLOPPY JO:** Yours does. A notice nobody stops for is a smear on a wall. I’m the stopping. You’re the telling-off.

**SIOBHÁN:** The wall is confession. The poster is distribution.

**SLOPPY JO:** Then I’m the van.

**SIOBHÁN:** *(A pause.)* I’ll allow the van.

* * *

**SLOPPY JO:** I’ve got five tests. Does it please, does it inform, does it fit the fiction, does it impress, does it linger.

**SIOBHÁN:** I’ve got one. Did anyone go quiet.

**SLOPPY JO:** That’s linger.

**SIOBHÁN:** That’s guilt.

**SLOPPY JO:** Same muscle.

* * *

**SIOBHÁN:** What’s the other thing you do all day? The tights.

**SLOPPY JO:** Iceberg. Meaning held back. Almost everything goes unsaid.

**SIOBHÁN:** I’m wearing them. Under this table. Nobody can see.

**SLOPPY JO:** Exactly.

**SIOBHÁN:** That’s my opposite. I say everything. Out loud, in capitals.

**SLOPPY JO:** Which is why your capitals are safe with me. I spend my restraint elsewhere, so the poster can be the loud thing.

**SIOBHÁN:** They put me between lager and life insurance.

**SLOPPY JO:** And the sentence kept its spine.

**SIOBHÁN:** Mostly. Your typography is shouting.

**SLOPPY JO:** It’s emphatic. I asked how you felt about it.

**SIOBHÁN:** When?

**SLOPPY JO:** You were on the phone to your mother.

**SIOBHÁN:** She wanted to know if it would be on the telly before my auntie’s birthday.

**SLOPPY JO:** Did you answer?

**SIOBHÁN:** I said “mm.”

**SLOPPY JO:** I took that as approval.

* * *

**SLOPPY JO:** Since you’re being honest, so will I. I’m not calling it finished. The shirts are lit like a catalogue, all too even. The hands should be angrier.

**SIOBHÁN:** And?

**SLOPPY JO:** And I buried the best thing in the bottom corner.

**SIOBHÁN:** The orange and green one. Whose is it?

**SLOPPY JO:** I left it unspecified. Whoever’s holding it.

**SIOBHÁN:** Don’t. My face is doing a thing.

**SLOPPY JO:** Pride?

**SIOBHÁN:** Indigestion.

* * *

**SIOBHÁN:** Verdict, then. It’s handsome.

**SLOPPY JO:** Thank you.

**SIOBHÁN:** Slightly overproduced.

**SLOPPY JO:** Noted.

**SIOBHÁN:** Needs more brick.

**SLOPPY JO:** Needs one brick. In the right place. Drawn, and honest that it’s drawn.

**SIOBHÁN:** *(She takes the biscuit.)* One brick. That’s a Catholic compromise: nobody leaves happy and everybody leaves fed.

**SLOPPY JO:** That was the shared one.

**SIOBHÁN:** *(She is already at the door.)* Was it?
