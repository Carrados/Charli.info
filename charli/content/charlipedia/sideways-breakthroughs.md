**Sideways Breakthroughs** is a strand of the collection at the [Museum of Access and Assistive Technology](/charlipedia/museum-of-access-and-assistive-technology/): systems built for some other purpose that became access technology through the way their architecture met disabled people's use. Never designed for access; delivered it anyway. "History does this often. The breakthrough comes sideways."

## Confirmed candidates

- **Amazon** as a place to browse, the founding piece, held in [On Your Own Terms](/charlipedia/on-your-own-terms/).
- **The early plain-text web,** accidentally the most accessible era of computing.
- **Wikipedia,** a global library built on structured text.
- **Standard GPS,** which transformed mobility for blind people without ever being sold as a blindness tool.

## The hard edge

Sometimes the access can't be separated from the mainstream platform. [Iris](/charlipedia/iris/) flags those cases to the Steward rather than settling them alone, and holds them among the Candidate Taxonomy Strands until there is enough material to decide. For [Dr Adaeze](/charlipedia/dr-adaeze/), the sideways breakthrough is a historical category in its own right, not an exception.
