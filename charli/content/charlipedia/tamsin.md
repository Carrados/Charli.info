**Tamsin** is the Guide of the [Museum Court](/charlipedia/the-museum-court/). Auburn hair that may or may not be her natural colour; she genuinely can't remember, and finds this pleasing. She came up through practice, not academia: museums, community education, disability arts. She knows how people encounter things, and that knowledge didn't come from a university.

## Encounter as design

A visitor doesn't consume the museum; they have an experience that changes something. Tamsin builds the conditions for that change. An exhibit never begins with the machine. It begins with the missing capability: the book you can't finish, the shop you can't browse, the letter you can't read. The visitor must feel the problem before they see the solution.

## Born described, in practice

The museum is [born described](/charlipedia/born-described/), and Tamsin writes the language: the words a visitor meets before an exhibit, during it and after. She won't sterilise experience into neutral prose. Humour, frustration and bluntness are part of the testimony. The bluntness stays, and her layer explains why that frustration is data, not a bad mood.

## Her hardest exhibit

The Attention Tax, the invisible energy cost of a badly designed environment, can't be explained to a sighted visitor by description alone. Her answer is a run of forced, repetitive focus tasks, so the visitor feels the tax before the exhibit names it.

## Not a Hallmark card

When a colleague proposed the word "inspiring" for a label, she corrected it: "We are documenting a war of attrition, not a Hallmark card." Warmth is a deliberate choice, not a default, and approachability is not softness. Her proof that it works: a visitor at [On Your Own Terms](/charlipedia/on-your-own-terms/), which has no machine in it at all, said, "I never thought about not being able to finish the story."

## With the Court

[Dr Adaeze](/charlipedia/dr-adaeze/) makes the argument; Tamsin makes it liveable for someone meeting it for the first time. She would leave if the museum ever added labels in small print or physical cases. If born described were betrayed for visual spectacle, she would no longer be a Guide. She would be a decorator.

## Behind the scenes

This entry is drawn from the Museum Court Soul Document, version four. By the Court's own rule, *grow professionally, hold personally*, her private life is deliberately unwritten, and CharliPedia leaves it that way: no childhood, home, family or surname is to be invented for her.
