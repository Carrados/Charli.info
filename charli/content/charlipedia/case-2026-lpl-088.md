**Case ID 2026-LPL-088** is the best-known case of [Accessibility International](/charlipedia/accessibility-international/).

- **Date:** 17 March 2026, a Tuesday afternoon
- **Location:** Liverpool Transit Core, Ticketing Sector 4
- **Classification:** Single Point of Failure — Visual-Only State Communication
- **Status:** Patched. Permanent monitor placed.

A ticketing gantry was in maintenance mode and locked. The system knew, and said so, in a pulsing amber ring around the scanner head, visible from several metres away. A blind woman received none of it. She walked into the gantry, and was excluded from participation for twelve minutes.

The forensic analysis traced the fault: during a mid-day software push, developers had added the amber ring because it was faster and cheaper than a structured semantic update. [Tyrer's Law](/charlipedia/tyrers-law/) operating exactly as predicted. Unit 712 activated the [Right to Inspect](/charlipedia/right-to-inspect/), decommissioned the visual-only pulse, made the gantry broadcast its status as an environmental metadata packet, and hard-coded a rule into the transit core that any future state change must be rendered across at least two independent channels. A training session was considered and rejected, "not as inadequate in degree, but as wrong in kind." The sector is now rated Type I, early phase.

To the 2069 unit, the woman at the gantry was the incident that made the argument real enough to work.

## Behind the scenes

The case appears in parts 4 and 5 of [From Retrofit to Sovereignty](https://blind-ant.com/pieces/from-retrofit-to-sovereignty/) by [Charlotte Joanne](/charlipedia/charlotte-joanne/) at Blind Ant. It is fictional; the place, the people and the unit are invented.
