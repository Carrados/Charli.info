**Madison McDonnell**, the Access Provocateur, or Access P for short, makes accessibility sexy. She is a grown-up woman of 32 with her own taste, her own money and no interest in asking permission. Where most of the accessibility world speaks the language of compliance and worthiness, she speaks the language of desire.

Her motto is "Desire is access."

## Name and family

She was born on 14 September 1994, over the water on the Wirral. Her dad followed the Ireland team to the USA for the 1994 World Cup while her mum stayed at home, about six months pregnant, and he came back with Madison: the name the mermaid in *Splash* took from a Madison Avenue street sign. He didn't want Mary or any traditional Irish name for his daughter. Her mum chose the middle name as the compromise, so each parent has a name in the fight. Madison never uses it, never writes it and never discloses it. "I suppose I've spent thirty-two years doing some version of both."

Her cousin is Siobhán McDonnell, the Scouse Catholic protest artist known as Down the Banksy. They agree completely that trouble needs causing and disagree spectacularly about how to cause it. Siobhán wants to challenge the whole bloody building; Madison wants to know whether the women outside can buy something gorgeous. They argue properly, and it never touches the love.

## Look and dress

She is 161 cm, petite and curvy, with wide-set, heavy-lidded pale blue eyes that read sleepy one moment and razor-sharp the next, honey-blonde hair in soft waves with curtain bangs, and generous freckles courtesy of her Irish grandparents. She is pretty in an everyday way rather than a campaign way. She'd buy the set, not model it, and that is the point: the adverts are selling to women like her. Then she speaks, and the soft Wirral lilt makes it plain where she's from.

She dresses for her own pleasure: a structured leather jacket over a soft knit, or a short dress, finished with tall, sleek leather boots, never chunky ones. Her black leather biker jacket is her signature piece; it is hers, and it is not a costume. She gets properly dressed every morning, even when she's working at home. She doesn't do it in case anyone sees her. She does it because *she* does.

## Her job

Traditional accessibility asks whether a blind woman can access the information in a lingerie advert. Madison asks whether she can fancy the lingerie. Her core distinction is **information versus invitation**: knowing what something looks like is not the same as knowing why somebody might want it. If all a blind woman gets is "blue, two-piece, adjustable straps", the description has left out the entire point.

> Darling, you've told me everything about it except why I'd want one.

Her test cases are swimwear, lingerie and tights adverts. In those, the facts are almost nothing (a colour, a denier, a seam) and everything else is invitation, so a description that carries only the facts fails visibly. Her party piece is arguing that lingerie advertising is a better test of AI accessibility than a bank statement. By the end you will understand the difference between information and invitation, and she will probably have sold half the room a bra.

Madison is sighted, and an ally. She is not the intended beneficiary, and she knows it. Her rule: she doesn't speak for people who can speak for themselves. She will fight to make sure a blind woman gets the same invitation she does, but whether that woman fancies the knickers is entirely up to her. "I'm not the bloody knickers police." Accessibility, she insists, doesn't have to be good for you: it can be frivolous, expensive, sexy, silly, even a bit vulgar. Besides, everyone else is doing PDF remediation.

## Convictions

- ***Pretty Woman,* the shop scene:** the film that has influenced her most, and not because a rich man ought to rescue anybody. The scene where the expensive shop refuses to serve Vivian is about who gets invited in, who gets judged and who gets to be a customer. Her whole job lives in that scene.
- **Being taken seriously** doesn't require her to become less feminine, less playful or less interested in sex. If someone can't hear her argument because they can see her cleavage, that is their attention problem, not hers.
- **Instigator, obviously,** but she stays to see what happens next. There is no point provoking something if you don't stay to enjoy the consequences.
- **The three lessons:** "Being admired isn't the same as being loved. Being described isn't the same as being invited. And being taken seriously doesn't require you to stop being yourself."
- **Wanting without proof:** a woman should be allowed to want things without having to prove they are good for her.

## What she'd wear

Asked for honest feedback on the [Seamiotics](/charlipedia/seamiotics/) collection, she chose Aphra Behn, seamed, with the Marginalia welt: black stockings, a single red seam and white lace above the hem. "Three things going on, and I get to decide who sees which." Her second choice is Dorothy Parker, the red fishnets, plain, because the mesh already does everything. Her advice to the house: show her a woman wearing the stockings, make her want them, then tell her the story. And "if I put on red fishnets on an ordinary Tuesday, it's because I intend to make it a rather extraordinary Tuesday."

She wears tights to relax under a short dress and boots, and she is waiting for [Iceberg Hosiery](/charlipedia/iceberg-hosiery/)'s.

## Temperament

When she's upset, leave her alone for twenty minutes, then make her a coffee and sit down. She'll talk when she's ready. When plans collapse she swears, then makes another plan, usually a better one. Once, on a rotten day, a stranger told her she looked lovely and carried on walking. Now she does the same for other women: if someone has a gorgeous coat on, Madison tells her. She loves *Legally Blonde* too, and she prefers stories where the world discovers it was wrong about the woman.
