---
title: Classical accessibility
description: Classical accessibility makes fixed, visually designed artefacts accessible after the fact, and is the permanent deterministic floor beneath everything else.
status: real
---

**Classical accessibility** is accessibility applied to fixed artefacts. A page is authored, a layout designed, a control placed, and then the artefact is made accessible with semantic markup, alt text, keyboard handlers and standards. The artefact exists in a known state, and the accessibility layer describes or adapts it.

For more than three decades it has carried real weight. Screen readers, WCAG, ARIA, semantic HTML and keyboard navigation are "an extraordinary collective achievement": the **deterministic floor** that prevents catastrophic information loss. Its necessity is permanent, and nothing generative replaces it.

## Its limit

It was built for a web of fixed objects at fixed addresses that looked the same to everyone: in the [Tyrer Framework](/charlipedia/tyrer-framework/)'s image, a Newtonian web. That web is disappearing. Interfaces are increasingly generated at the moment of use, and the artefact classical accessibility was designed to adapt no longer exists by the time the user arrives. Within its paradigm, visual first and adaptation second, the best it can achieve is the top of Type 0 on the [Tyrer Accessibility Scale](/charlipedia/tyrer-accessibility-scale/). Classical accessibility fails loudly, though: a missing label can be found and fixed. Its successor, [generative accessibility](/charlipedia/generative-accessibility/), can fail silently.
